Executive Summary & Abstract

Food waste represents an enormous ecological and economic crisis globally. Household kitchens are responsible for over 60% of all edible food waste, throwing away millions of tons of cooked rice, rotis, leftover curries, stale bread, and near-expiry vegetables. The primary driver of this waste is not intentional neglect, but rather a lack of immediate recipe inspiration—individuals often do not know how to assemble simple, appetizing meals using only three or four mismatched leftover ingredients without making unnecessary grocery purchases.

To solve this real-world problem, SmartBite was engineered as an individual full-stack web application. The platform features an intelligent, multi-parameter Leftover Matching Algorithm that normalizes available leftovers, handles plurals and Indian/Continental culinary synonyms, distinguishes core ingredients from common pantry staples (such as cooking oil and salt), and calculates percentage match accuracy. In addition, the system provides a 7-day scheduled meal planner, a fridge/pantry inventory tracker with expiry risk indicators, a smart missing-grocery checklist with auto-population, and an environmental/cost-saving impact tracker.

Chapter 1: Problem Statement & Real-World Motivation

1.1 The Global and Domestic Food Waste Crisis

According to the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) Food Waste Index Report, over 1 billion tons of food is wasted annually across the globe, contributing to 8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions (predominantly methane from anaerobic organic decomposition in landfills). In domestic households, consumer surveys indicate that the highest volume of discarded food consists of cooked surplus meals:

  • Leftover Cooked Grains: Boiled white/brown rice, khichdi, and cooked pasta that dry out in the refrigerator.
  • Surplus Cooked Dal & Curries: Dal tadka, sambar, and dry vegetable sabzis (paneer, aloo, mixed veg).
  • Dry Flatbreads & Bakery: Day-old chapatis/rotis, bread crusts, and stale loaf slices.
  • Perishable Produce: Softening tomatoes, single carrots, half-onions, and overripe bananas.

1.2 Root Cause Analysis

Traditional recipe platforms (such as generic cooking blogs and food apps) are structured around grocery shopping: they dictate an exhaustive list of 12–15 ingredients and expect the user to purchase missing specialty items. In contrast, when a working professional, student, or homemaker looks into their refrigerator at dinner time, they face the reverse problem:

"I already have a bowl of cooked rice, one onion, and two eggs. What can I cook immediately in 12 minutes without stepping out to the supermarket?"

1.3 Project Objectives

  1. Reverse Recipe Discovery: Allow users to enter 1 to N leftover ingredients and receive dynamically ranked recipe suggestions based on match percentage.
  2. Staple-Aware Matching: Differentiate critical main leftovers from universal kitchen staples (oil, salt, water, basic spices) so users are not penalized with low match scores.
  3. Preventative Planning: Provide a 7-Day Meal Scheduler so families can assign leftover transformations throughout the week.
  4. Smart Grocery Integration: Automatically transfer missing ingredients to a checklist so users buy only what is necessary.
  5. Tangible Impact Tracking: Quantify kg of food waste diverted and financial savings in INR to motivate sustainable household behavior.

Chapter 5: Major Modules & Leftover Matcher Algorithm

5.1 The Leftover Matching Algorithm

The core innovation of SmartBite is the algorithmic matching pipeline implemented in backend/app/routes/recipes.py:

  1. String Normalization: Input ingredient tokens are trimmed, lowercased, and mapped through a synonym dictionary (e.g., "eggs" → "egg", "rotis" → "roti", "chapatis" → "roti", "leftover rice" → "cooked rice").
  2. Database Candidate Retrieval: Active recipes matching any dietary or max-cooking-time filters are fetched.
  3. Ingredient Partitioning: For each candidate recipe, ingredients are separated into:
    • Matched Items: Ingredients the user already possesses.
    • Missing Non-Staples: Crucial missing items (e.g., carrots, cheese).
    • Pantry Staples: Common items (salt, oil, water) assumed to be present.
  4. Match Percentage Calculation:
    Match % = round((Count of Matched Core Ingredients / Total Core Ingredients) * 100)
  5. Relevance Sorting: Recipes are sorted in descending order of match_percentage, with secondary sorting by lowest preparation time.

5.2 Core Functional Modules

  • Module 1: Leftover Matcher: Interactive tag cloud, one-click demo presets, and live ranked recipe cards.
  • Module 2: Recipe Explorer & Modal: Step-by-step instructions, nutritional info (calories, prep time), and portion counts.
  • Module 3: 7-Day Meal Planner: Full weekly calendar grid (Monday–Sunday) for Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, and Snacks.
  • Module 4: Fridge & Pantry Tracker: Visual freshness badges (red for ≤ 1 day, amber for ≤ 3 days) with 1-click "Match All Fridge Items".
  • Module 5: Smart Grocery Checklist: Auto-populates missing recipe items and allows check-off upon purchase.
  • Module 6: Environmental & Cost Savings: Dynamic counters for food waste diverted (kg), money saved (₹), and CO₂e averted.

8.3 Model Answers to Examiner Questions

Q1: Why did you use SQLite instead of MongoDB?
"SQLite is an embedded, zero-configuration relational database stored directly inside the project directory. It requires zero server setup for the examiner, supports ACID transactions, and enforces foreign key cascading."

Q2: How did you implement two-tier validation?
"Client-side validation in React provides immediate feedback on empty inputs and non-negative values. Server-side validation uses Pydantic schemas in FastAPI to enforce exact field ranges, valid Enums, and automatic HTTP 422 error responses."

Q3: How does the ingredient matching algorithm work?
"The algorithm normalizes input strings, handles plurals and synonyms (e.g. roti/chapati), separates core leftovers from common pantry staples (salt, oil), and computes percentage match accuracy."
